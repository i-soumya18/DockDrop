# Research and synthesis

Read at step 2. Covers the `user-research` and `research-synthesis` disciplines.

## Be honest about what Apple does, then adapt

Secondhand accounts of Apple's process (former employees, books, conference talks) describe a culture that leans on first principles, taste, rapid prototyping and heavy internal use of the product more than on external research. One widely cited account describes demos shown early and often, with leaders making design calls in the room, and "living on the product" (using it daily) as a core habit. Another describes designers producing about ten very different mockups, narrowing to a few, then one. Treat these as reported practice, not documented policy, and note that even their own authors question whether the approach is replicable without Apple's concentration of design talent.

**Implication for most teams:** you probably lack Apple's taste density, so substitute small, fast research for it. Keep Apple's habits (prototype early, use the product daily, show work constantly) and add the cheap evidence Apple may not need.

## Match research to stage

| Stage | Question | Method | Size / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | Is there a real problem and who has it? | Interviews on current behavior (not on your idea) | 5-8 people, 1-2 weeks |
| Concept | Does this direction make sense? | Concept reaction, card sort for IA | 5-8 / 15-30 |
| Wireframe or prototype | Can people complete the key task? | Moderated usability test, think-aloud | 5-8 per round |
| Built | What do people actually do? | Analytics, session review, diary study | ongoing |
| Live | What is hurting retention? | Support tickets, reviews, NPS comments | continuous |

For a solo founder: two rounds of five 20-minute prototype tests beat one big study. Always test the *task*, never ask "do you like it".

## Interview rules that keep the data honest

- Ask about the last time they did the thing, not what they would do.
- Never describe your solution before they describe their problem.
- Watch behavior and workarounds; they outweigh stated opinions.
- Record exact quotes. Separate **observation** ("4 of 6 tapped the wrong tab") from **interpretation** ("the tab labels are unclear").

## Synthesis workflow

1. Dump raw observations, one per note, tagged with participant ID.
2. Affinity-group into themes; name themes as findings, not topics ("People abandon at the permissions prompt", not "Permissions").
3. Count prevalence: "5 of 8", never "most".
4. Convert each theme into an **insight to opportunity to design decision** row, with impact and effort.
5. Record what you still do not know; this becomes the next study.

Use the `research-synthesis` output template when available. Always include quotes and a methodology-limits note.

## When there are no users yet

State the riskiest assumptions, rank them by (impact if wrong) x (uncertainty), and design the smallest test for the top one: a clickable prototype, a landing page, a concierge version done by hand. Say clearly that findings from this are directional.

## Output of this step

A one-page **research brief**: top assumptions, method and sample per assumption, what decision each result will change, and (once data exists) the synthesis. Research that does not change a design decision is not finished; tie every finding to a screen or flow.

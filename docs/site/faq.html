FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Honest answers. No marketing speak.

General
What is DockDrop? ⌄

DockDrop is an Android app that backs up your phone to an external SSD you own. Plug in the SSD, tap once, and your photos, videos and documents are copied, SHA-256 verified, and sealed. No cloud, no subscription, no account needed.

Is DockDrop free? ⌄

Yes. DockDrop is free, with no in-app purchases, no premium tier, no subscription. It’s open source under Apache 2.0. There are no ads and no analytics.

Do I need an account? ⌄

No. DockDrop has no accounts, no sign-in, no cloud sync. Your backup lives on your SSD. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Which Android versions are supported? ⌄

DockDrop supports Android 8.0 (API 26) and above. It targets Android 15 (API 35). The reference test device is a Samsung Galaxy A14 running One UI 7.0 / Android 15. Samsung battery optimization behavior is specifically addressed in the onboarding.

Hardware & Compatibility
Which SSDs work? ⌄

Any USB-C external SSD or flash drive formatted as exFAT (recommended) or FAT32 should work. DockDrop was verified on a Hikvision 128 GB exFAT SSD on a Samsung Galaxy A14.

NTFS drives often appear as read-only on Android — DockDrop will detect this and show an amber warning. Reformatting to exFAT resolves it. FAT32 works but has a 4 GB file size limit. DockDrop checks write access with a test write before starting any backup.

Do I need a USB OTG adapter? ⌄

If your phone and SSD both have USB-C, a direct cable usually works. If your SSD is USB-A, you’ll need a USB-A to USB-C OTG adapter. DockDrop doesn’t require any specific adapter — it uses Android’s standard USB host stack.

How fast is the backup? ⌄

Measured on a Samsung Galaxy A14 with a Hikvision exFAT SSD via USB-C:

  • Sequential write (1 GB): 35.9 MB/s
  • Small files (2,000 × 100 KB): 2.24 MB/s / 23 files/s
  • Mixed real data (2.4 GB): 14.6 MB/s / 14 files/s

A first 20 GB backup (copy + SHA-256 on copy + re-read) takes roughly 23–38 minutes. Subsequent backups of unchanged data complete in seconds (zero bytes copied).

Backup & Restore
Can I back up WhatsApp messages and chats? ⌄

No. DockDrop can back up WhatsApp Media (photos, videos, documents sent/received) from Android/media/com.whatsapp/WhatsApp/Media. However, WhatsApp’s chat database, message history, and call logs live in Android/data/com.whatsapp/, which Android blocks all third-party apps from accessing. This is an Android OS restriction, not a DockDrop limitation. The same applies to every app’s private data folder.

What happens if I pull the cable mid-backup? ⌄

DockDrop writes a journal checkpoint and posts a notification: “Backup interrupted at 63%. Plug the SSD back in to resume.” When you reconnect, DockDrop reads the journal and picks up from where it stopped. The final result is byte-identical to a backup that ran uninterrupted. No .part files are left behind. The committed manifest generation from the previous run remains valid and intact.

What does “Sealed” mean? What’s a Receipt? ⌄

Sealed means every planned file was: written via temp-file + rename (atomic), size-matched on write, SHA-256 hashed during copy, re-read from the SSD and the hash matched, and the manifest generation was committed with a checksum. Only then does DockDrop show the green check.

The Receipt is a summary card showing: number of files, total size, duration, and the exact verification level used (e.g. “size + SHA-256 on copy + re-read”). It always says “Nothing was deleted from your phone.” You can share it as plain text.

Can I restore to a different phone? ⌄

Yes. Install DockDrop on the new phone, plug in the SSD, and DockDrop will detect the backup automatically and offer to restore it. It maps each source folder to the new phone’s folder structure and guides you through granting permissions. You can also restore without DockDrop at all by opening the plain folders on any laptop.

Does DockDrop delete anything from my phone? ⌄

Never. A Drop never modifies or deletes anything on your phone. DockDrop is read-only on the phone side. The Receipt always confirms: “Nothing was deleted from your phone.”

What happens to files I delete from my phone? ⌄

By default (deletion policy: “Keep them on the SSD”), files deleted from your phone are moved to a _deleted/<SetName>/<date>/ folder on your SSD. They are never removed unless you choose “Mirror exactly” mode or set a “Keep for N days” policy. Mirror mode has a mass-delete guard: if more than 20% of a Set’s files or 500 files would be removed in one run, the Drop stops and shows you the list first.

Privacy & Security
Does DockDrop connect to the internet? ⌄

No. Never. DockDrop declares no INTERNET permission in its AndroidManifest.xml. Android’s permission system makes it impossible for the app to open network connections. This is enforced automatically — a CI lint check fails the build if INTERNET or ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE ever appear. You can verify: Settings → Apps → DockDrop → Permissions.

Is encryption available? ⌄

Yes, as an optional per-Set feature. DockDrop uses AES-256-GCM chunked streaming encryption with scrypt/Argon2id key derivation. You set a passphrase and get a recovery key. Both are required to restore an encrypted backup on a new device. DockDrop tells you this upfront — if you lose both, the backup cannot be opened.

Encryption is off by default. Unencrypted backups are plain folders readable on any laptop without DockDrop.

Is DockDrop open source? ⌄

Yes. DockDrop is open source under the Apache 2.0 license. You can read every line of code, the AndroidManifest.xml, and the CI configuration on GitHub.

Troubleshooting
The backup stopped after a while. Why? ⌄

Android limits certain foreground services to 6 hours per 24-hour period. If DockDrop approaches the limit, it saves a journal checkpoint and posts a “Continue backup?” notification. Tap it to resume from exactly where it stopped. On Samsung devices with aggressive battery optimization, you may need to follow the in-app Samsung battery guidance (Settings → Keep running) to let DockDrop finish with the screen off.

My SSD shows as read-only. What do I do? ⌄

This usually means the SSD is formatted as NTFS, which Android can read but not write. DockDrop detects this and shows an amber warning. Reformat the SSD to exFAT on your computer — it works on Windows, Mac, and Android. exFAT has no file size limits and is the recommended format for external SSDs used with Android.

DockDrop didn’t detect my SSD. How do I trigger a backup? ⌄

If the automatic USB notification didn’t appear, check that you granted DockDrop USB device access in the system dialog. You can also trigger a backup manually using the Quick Settings tile (“Back up now”) or by opening DockDrop directly and tapping “Back up now” from the home screen.

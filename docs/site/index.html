What makes it different

Built on trust,
not just convenience

Most backup apps copy your files. DockDrop proves they arrived intact — every single time.

✓

Verified, not just copied

A backup is only Sealed when every planned file was written via temp-file + rename, size-matched, SHA-256 checked, and the manifest was committed. The Receipt names the verification level actually used.

SHA-256 on copy + re-read
⚡

One tap. Done.

Plug in your SSD. A notification appears. Tap Start backup. Lock your phone. DockDrop keeps running in the background.

🔒

Fully offline by design

DockDrop has no internet permission — not just “no cloud”. Android's permission system enforces it. You can verify it yourself in Settings → Apps → DockDrop → Permissions.

≡

No lock-in. Plain folders.

Unencrypted backups are plain folders on your SSD — open them on any laptop, any OS, without DockDrop. The format is documented and versioned from day one.

↻

Restore is first-class

Hold to restore everything, browse by folder, restore single files, or set up a new phone from your backup. Conflicts are previewed before anything is written.

⚡

Atomic and resumable

Pull the cable at any point. Plug back in. DockDrop resumes from where it stopped. No corrupted files, no partial writes left behind.

How it works

From plug-in to
Sealed in one tap

1
Plug in your SSD

DockDrop detects your drive in under 300 ms. A notification appears: “Tap to back up.”

2
Tap Start backup

The backup only starts on your tap — never automatically. Lock your phone; it keeps going.

3
Watch the live flow

Files stream from your phone to the SSD. Progress shows folder by folder, speed and ETA in real time.

4
Receive your Receipt

When every file is verified, a Receipt prints: files, size, duration, verification level. Then: “Safe to unplug.”

Privacy

Offline isn’t a feature.
It’s the product.

DockDrop has zero network permissions. Not “optional”, not “just analytics” — none. A CI lint check fails the build if INTERNET or ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE appear in the manifest. You can verify this yourself.

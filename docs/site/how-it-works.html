How it works

From plug-in to
Sealed in one tap

No setup ritual after the first run. Every backup is verified before it’s called done.

First time

Set up in 2 minutes

1

Choose what to protect

DockDrop suggests the folders that matter most: Camera, Pictures, WhatsApp Media, Documents. Tap to grant access. Each one shows its size estimate. You can add any sub-folder too.

Note: Android 15 doesn’t allow picking the entire Downloads folder root. DockDrop explains this and guides you to pick sub-folders instead.

2

Choose your SSD

Plug in your external SSD. DockDrop shows its name, capacity ring, filesystem (exFAT recommended), and runs a write test — a 16 MB temp file that gives you your actual speed. This feed your first ETA estimate.

Write test: 38 MB/s measured
3

Set the rules

Three decisions, all with safe defaults: Deletion policy (keep deleted files on SSD — default), Encryption (off by default; AES-256 available), Battery threshold (20% default). Each shows a plain-language consequence: “Result: deleted files move to _deleted/ on your SSD.”

4

Name your Set and create it

Give the backup Set a name (“My Phone” by default). Review the summary: folders, Dock, location, policy. Estimate: “First backup: about 14.2 GB · roughly 9–14 min.”

Tap Create backup set. If the SSD is plugged in, you’re asked: “Start the first backup now?”

Every time after

Plug. Tap. Done.

1

SSD detected <300 ms

When you plug in your SSD, DockDrop receives a USB_DEVICE_ATTACHED broadcast in under 300 ms — verified on a Samsung Galaxy A14, even when the app is swiped away or the screen is off.

A notification appears: “Samsung T7 detected · Tap to back up My Phone”. The backup never starts without your tap.

2

Tap “Start backup”

The plug-in sheet rises from the bottom with the SSD details — name, free space, filesystem. Below it: your Set name, how many files have changed, estimated time. Tap the button.

You can lock your phone immediately. DockDrop runs as a foreground service and keeps the notification visible, showing live progress.

3

Live Drop

The Drop screen shows files flowing from your phone to the SSD. Each folder segment lights up as it completes. Speed, ETA (smoothed, shown as a range when throughput is volatile), and a “files/s” counter are always visible.

Pull the cable? DockDrop marks the journal checkpoint and posts: “Backup interrupted at 63%. Plug the SSD back in to resume.”

35.9 MB/s median · SHA-256 computed during copy
4

Verify

After all folders are copied, DockDrop re-reads every file from the SSD and compares SHA-256 hashes. This pass runs after the full copy so it doesn’t read from Linux’s VFS page cache in RAM (which would give a false “pass”).

5

Sealed — receipt prints

The ring closes. A green checkmark draws. Your Receipt “prints” up from the Dock: files, size, duration, verification level used, confirmation that nothing was deleted from your phone.

“All writes finished. Safe to unplug.”

Edge cases

What happens when things go wrong

DockDrop is designed for the real world, not the happy path only.

⚠

SSD is full

DockDrop stops and shows exactly how much space is needed vs. how much is free. Nothing is corrupted — files copied so far are intact. You can choose fewer folders and continue.

⚠

Permission lost

If Android revokes a folder’s permission mid-backup, DockDrop pauses and shows which folder needs re-granting. Nothing is lost. The journal remembers where it was.

⚠

Mass-delete guard

In Mirror mode: if more than 20% of a Set’s files, or more than 500 files, would be removed in one Drop, the Drop stops and shows you the list before removing anything.

⚠

Service timeout

Android limits background services to 6 hours/day. If DockDrop is approaching the limit, it saves a journal checkpoint and posts “Continue backup?” — tap to resume from exactly where it stopped.
